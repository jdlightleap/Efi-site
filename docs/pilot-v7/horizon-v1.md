# Horizon research v1 (EFI UK Power Networks pilot)

Generated 29 Oct 2026 from the pilot data (`design/v6-ukpn-pilot/build/v6data.py`). This is the evidence base for the six Horizon cards. It replaces the old internal catalogue references on the cards. Public sources only. Draft for review, not endorsed by UK Power Networks.

Evidence grades: **announcement**: Announcement: a plan or order, nothing running yet; **demo**: Demo: shown working once or in trials, no results at scale; **pilot**: Pilot: running with real users, under supervision; **commercial**: Commercial: on sale and in routine use

## H1 · Humanoid field robots: Humanoid robots doing substation rounds and switching

- Evidence grade: **demo** · Time horizon: 5–10 years · Proven in energy?: B2 (Shown on a similar problem)
- Why it matters: China's biggest grid company is training humanoid robots on real substation tasks, including switching. If it works, robots could take on jobs that need hands, not just cameras. Nobody has published how well they do yet.
- Why now: Training at a live 110 kV substation (Jul 2026); China consulting on a standard
- Breakthrough: Robot AI models can now run a whole humanoid body from one model. Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 (Jul 2026) is one, but its own figures show fine hand tasks often fail.
- Nearest proof in energy: State Grid Hangzhou is training humanoids for switching at the 110 kV Xiaohe substation. A partner firm names infrared temperature checks, switchgear operation and earthing as target tasks.
- GB position: UK Power Networks trialled a robot dog in cable tunnels (2023). Several GB robotics projects use wheeled, legged or flying robots; none uses humanoids.
- Abroad: China: training trials at a State Grid substation; a national standard is being drafted.
- Evidence gap: No success rates, supervision levels or failure data have been published from any substation trial.
- First objection: This is hype. Tesla's own boss said its Optimus robots were not yet doing useful work. Answer: Agreed that it's early. That's why this is Horizon, not a project: the session sets signposts and triggers, not a budget.

Evidence so far:

- 23 Jul 2026: State Grid Hangzhou is training humanoid robots for switching at a 110 kV substation. Source: [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/energy/20260723/85e60523f53049baad2db96057ff9108/c.html)
- Jul 2026: UBTECH and a grid-equipment partner name infrared checks, switchgear operation and earthing as target tasks. Source: [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103265696.html)
- 22 Jun 2026: China's industry ministry consulted on plans for a standard for humanoid robots, including in substations. Source: [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260622/36b380f1ef27491c97f6e67f6b726a29/c.html)
- 30 Jul 2026: Gemini Robotics 2: one model controls a whole humanoid. Results are the company's own. Source: [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-brings-whole-body-intelligence-to-robots/)
- Jul 2026: RoboDojo benchmark: the best public model completed 12.8% of attempted tasks. Source: [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04434)
- 28 Jan 2026: Reality check: Elon Musk said Tesla's Optimus robots were not yet doing useful work in its factories. Source: [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/01/28/musk-admits-no-optimus-robots-are-doing-useful-work-at-tesla-after-claiming-otherwise/)

## H2 · Space data centres: Data centres in orbit, powered by constant sunlight

- Evidence grade: **demo** · Time horizon: 10–15 years · Proven in energy?: B1 (Analogy only (weak))
- Why it matters: AI firms are testing data centres in orbit, where solar power is almost constant. Even the optimists put cost parity in the mid-2030s. For networks, it's a long-range question about how much data-centre demand stays on the ground.
- Why now: Google's prototype launched 1 Oct 2026; Starcloud raised $250m (Aug 2026)
- Breakthrough: Starcloud-1 has run an Nvidia data-centre chip in orbit since Nov 2025 and trained a small AI model there.
- Nearest proof in energy: Google's analysis says launching solar-powered compute could match US data-centre power costs if launch prices fall to about $200 per kg, which it thinks could happen by the mid-2030s.
- GB position: No GB network work found.
- Abroad: US: Starcloud and Google prototypes in orbit.
- Evidence gap: Chips overheat and must power down every 15–20 minutes on Google's prototype; radiation causes silent errors. Nobody has run a useful workload at scale.
- First objection: Data centres are old news, and space ones are science fiction. Answer: Ground data centres are old news. The new question is whether some of that load leaves the ground in the 2030s. The session decides whether that's worth one line in a forecast.

Evidence so far:

- 10 Dec 2025: Starcloud-1 trained a small AI model in orbit on an Nvidia chip. Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/10/nvidia-backed-starcloud-trains-first-ai-model-in-space-orbital-data-centers.html)
- 21 Aug 2026: Starcloud raised $250m. Next two satellites (8 kW each) fly in 2027; bigger ones depend on SpaceX's Starship. Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/21/starcloud-raises-200-million-for-orbital-data-centers-as-launch-options-dry-up/)
- 1 Oct 2026: Google's Project Suncatcher prototype, built with Planet, is in orbit and “operating as expected”. Source: [Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/project-suncatcher-prototype/)
- 1 Oct 2026: Prototype runs 4 AI chips on about 1 kW. Chips power down every 15–20 minutes due to heat. Cost parity needs about $200 per kg launch, perhaps by the mid-2030s. Source: [Payload](https://payloadspace.com/transporter-18-tests-the-building-blocks-of-orbital-data-centers/)

## H3 · Space-based solar power: Solar power collected in space and beamed to the ground

- Evidence grade: **demo** · Time horizon: 10–15 years · Proven in energy?: B2 (Shown on a similar problem)
- Why it matters: Satellites that collect sunlight and beam it down could one day give round-the-clock power to a receiving site. So far only a trace has reached the ground. A first test aiming for tens of watts is under way.
- Why now: Cowboy Space's Reason-1 launched 1 Oct 2026; beaming attempt due
- Breakthrough: In 2023 Caltech's MAPLE beamed power wirelessly in space and sent a detectable signal to Earth.
- Nearest proof in energy: Cowboy Space (formerly Aetherflux) launched Reason-1 on 1 Oct 2026, aiming to send 30–100 W by laser to a ground spot 10–20 m wide. No result reported by 29 Oct 2026.
- GB position: No GB network work found. A UK company, Space Solar, has a letter of intent (not a contract) with an Icelandic utility for 30 MW by 2030.
- Abroad: US: Caltech demo (2023), Cowboy Space test (2026). Iceland: letter of intent.
- Evidence gap: No orbit-to-ground power result yet. Costs and safety at grid scale are unknown.
- First objection: A hundred watts is a light bulb. This is decades away. Answer: Yes. The session is about signposts and what a receiver connection would look like, not about buying power.

Evidence so far:

- 1 Jun 2023: Caltech's MAPLE transmitted power wirelessly in space and beamed detectable power to Earth. Source: [Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/in-a-first-caltechs-space-solar-power-demonstrator-wirelessly-transmits-power-in-space)
- 1 Oct 2026: Reason-1 launched; it aims to beam 30–100 W to a ground spot. No result reported yet. Source: [Payload](https://payloadspace.com/transporter-18-tests-the-building-blocks-of-orbital-data-centers/)
- 1 Oct 2026: Cowboy Space's own launch note. Source: [Cowboy Space](https://www.cowboyspace.com/news/reason-1-takes-flight)
- 29 Oct 2024: Space Solar signed a letter of intent with Reykjavik Energy for 30 MW by 2030. A letter of intent is not a contract. Source: [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2024/10/29/space-solar-secures-letter-of-intent-from-reykjavik-energy/)
- 2026: UK government-commissioned report on space solar pathways to net zero. Source: [GOV.UK](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/698f167c7da91680ad7f43ad/SBSP-enabled-pathways-to-net-zero-final-report-raf036-2425.pdf)

## H4 · Fusion and superconducting tape: Fusion's first grid connections, and the superconducting cable it brings

- Evidence grade: **announcement** · Time horizon: 10–15 years · Proven in energy?: B2 (Shown on a similar problem)
- Why it matters: A US fusion firm has applied to connect a 400 MW plant for the early 2030s, and Britain's prototype plant is planned for 2040. Fusion connects at high voltage, so the nearer benefit for local networks is the superconducting tape it is buying at scale, which also makes compact city cables.
- Why now: First fusion grid application (Apr 2026); record tape order (Sep 2026)
- Breakthrough: Commonwealth Fusion Systems ordered more than 10,000 km of superconducting tape from Fujikura (Sep 2026). The firm calls it the largest such order; that is its own claim.
- Nearest proof in energy: The same high-temperature superconducting tape is used in compact power cables, which GB networks studied in SCADENT (2022).
- GB position: SCADENT (2022, led by National Grid with UK Power Networks and others) studied superconducting cables. STEP, the UK prototype fusion plant at West Burton, is planned to operate in 2040.
- Abroad: US: first fusion grid application (PJM). Japan: tape maker scaling.
- Evidence gap: Fusion has not yet produced net electricity. Tape price per metre after the big orders is not public.
- First objection: Fusion is always 30 years away. Answer: We aren't betting on fusion power. Fusion's tape orders may make superconducting cables cheaper sooner; that's the signpost to watch.

Evidence so far:

- 28 Apr 2026: Commonwealth Fusion Systems applied to connect a 400 MW fusion plant in Virginia to the PJM grid; studies can take 4–6 years. Source: [CFS](https://cfs.energy/news-and-media/commonwealth-fusion-systems-becomes-first-fusion-company-to-apply-to-pjm-interconnection-the-largest-u.s.-wholesale-electricity-market)
- 16 Mar 2026: UK's STEP fusion plant at West Burton appointed a £200m construction partner; operation planned for 2040. Source: [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ilios-appointment-speeds-delivery-of-step-fusion-at-west-burton)
- 30 Sep 2026: Fujikura order from CFS for more than 10,000 km of superconducting tape (company says largest). Source: [Fujikura](https://www.fujikura.co.jp/en/news/pressrelease/20260930cfsarc1km.html)
- Mar–May 2022: SCADENT studied superconducting cables for dense networks; UK Power Networks was a funding partner. Source: [ENA Innovation Portal](https://smarter.energynetworks.org/projects/10027601/)

## H5 · Robotaxi fleets on the grid: Robotaxi fleets as big, flexible loads and future grid batteries

- Evidence grade: **pilot** · Time horizon: 5–10 years · Proven in energy?: B2 (Shown on a similar problem)
- Why it matters: Robotaxis are in London with safety drivers. Fleets charge at depots on a timetable a computer sets, which could make them large, flexible loads, and their old batteries could become grid storage.
- Why now: London robotaxi rides began 3 Sep 2026
- Breakthrough: Driverless taxis run commercially in parts of the US, China and the UAE.
- Nearest proof in energy: Waymo announced (Jun 2026) that retired fleet batteries will become grid storage, “hundreds of megawatts”, starting in Texas and California.
- GB position: Wayve and Uber started supervised rides in London; Waymo and Baidu are testing there.
- Abroad: US, China and UAE: driverless services. US: fleet batteries to grid storage (announced).
- Evidence gap: No public data yet on depot sizes, charging patterns or fleet sizes in London.
- First objection: Driverless taxis in London are years away. Answer: Driverless approval looks unlikely in 2026. Depots and charging will be planned before then, which is when network choices get made.

Evidence so far:

- 3 Sep 2026: Wayve and Uber began supervised robotaxi rides in London with about 15 cars, each with a safety driver. Source: [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/03/london-first-self-driving-taxis-for-hire-wayve-uber)
- 4 Jun 2026: Waymo and B2U will reuse retired fleet batteries as grid storage, starting in Texas and California. Source: [Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/06/b2u-partnership/)

## H6 · Quantum for grid switching: Quantum computers helping reconfigure the network

- Evidence grade: **demo** · Time horizon: 10–15 years · Proven in energy?: B3 (Shown on a power-system problem)
- Why it matters: A US city utility used its own quantum computer to find better ways to reconfigure its network. It matched good answers on test networks, but overall it was not yet faster than ordinary computers.
- Why now: EPB and IonQ results published 28 Oct 2026
- Breakthrough: EPB (Chattanooga) runs an IonQ quantum computer on site, funded partly by a $4m US government grant.
- Nearest proof in energy: On a 417-node test network, quantum hints cut a classical solver's time from 1,415 seconds to 35 seconds, but quantum overhead dominated end-to-end time on small and medium networks.
- GB position: No GB network quantum-optimisation project found. NESO is working on quantum-safe security, which is a different topic.
- Abroad: US: EPB and IonQ.
- Evidence gap: No end-to-end speed-up shown; results are preprints.
- First objection: Quantum is all hype. Answer: Partly. These are preprints, not peer-reviewed, and not faster overall. That's why the move is a benchmark, not a purchase.

Evidence so far:

- 28 Oct 2026: EPB and IonQ published three preprints on network reconfiguration on IonQ Forte hardware. Source: [Quantum Computing Report](https://quantumcomputingreport.com/epb-and-ionq-deploy-hybrid-trapped-ion-algorithms-on-forte-enterprise-to-optimize-power-grid-topology/)
- Oct 2026: Preprint: quantum-guided solver on networks up to 417 nodes. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40264)
